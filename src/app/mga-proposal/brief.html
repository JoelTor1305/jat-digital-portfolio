MGA growth & call automation pilot

How we’ll measure service improvements, lead recovery, and ROI. Prepared for Roger, before any build begins.

01The goal

The goal is a measurable return, with data that backs whether the system is actually useful. Before MGA commits to a wider build, the pilot has to show real numbers: how many calls it handled, how many became trials, and what that’s worth. If the data shows it works, we expand. If it doesn’t, we know exactly why.

We’re starting deliberately small: a few things done well, with low risk, so the results are clean and easy to judge.

02What we measure

Not every call is a sale. A parent asking for directions was never a trial, so judging the agent on “did it convert” would punish it for doing its job well. Instead, every call gets sorted into one of three types, and each type is measured differently:

Service

Routine questions: pricing, schedules, directions, policies. Measured on speed and how many it resolves without staff. See section 3.

Revenue

Lead capture and trial scheduling. Measured on how many turn into trials, enrollments, and dollars. See section 4.

Escalation

Not for the agent: billing, cancellations, policy exceptions, upset parents. Measured on how cleanly it hands these to staff.

03Service quality metrics

For service calls, “better, faster, more efficient” turned into numbers we track:

05How we capture the data

Every call the agent handles gets logged as one row in a single tracking sheet. The category column is what makes the whole thing measurable: it sorts each call into service, revenue, or escalation, so the numbers in sections 3 and 4 can be pulled straight from the log. These are the columns we capture on each call:

ColumnWhat it captures
Date / timeWhen the call came in
CallerName and contact, if given
CategoryServiceRevenueEscalation
Question / needWhat the caller actually wanted
OutcomeAnswered, lead captured, trial link sent, trial booked, or escalated to staff
Trial requestedYes / no
Trial bookedYes / no
Staff follow-upWhether a human still had to step in
Recovered callWhether this call would have gone to voicemail otherwise

That single sheet becomes the dashboard: calls answered, trials scheduled, and conversions in one place, instead of scattered across five systems. The “category” and “recovered call” columns are what let us separate routine service from real revenue activity, and tell which results the agent is actually responsible for.

The part that keeps paying after the pilot

Answering the phone is the easy half. The real asset is that every call becomes structured data, and after 60 to 90 days that data shows you things most gyms never get to see. Data only matters if it changes a decision, so here is the decision each one drives:

Most common questions → Put the answers on the website and kill those calls entirely
Best lead sources → Move ad spend toward what actually brings enrollments
Missed-call volume → Know exactly how many leads were leaking before, in dollars
Staff workload → Free the team from repetitive calls for higher-value work

06What the pilot covers

What the agent does
+Morning coverage to start, limited hours
+Answers repetitive FAQs: pricing, schedules, directions, camp, birthday parties, registration fees, makeup policies
+Captures lead information from callers
+Sends a post-call email automatically, at minimum the trial request form link
+Notifies staff and logs notes for follow-up
+Logs call info into one spreadsheet so everything is in one place
What it deliberately leaves out
−No automatic Jackrabbit booking. A human verifies before anything is booked
−No billing disputes, cancellations, policy exceptions, upset parents, or competitive concerns
−Not always-on, and not a full front-desk replacement

07Where it can go later

Once the pilot proves out, these are the natural next steps, each built only after the last is working and trusted:

Direct class booking

The system sees open classes and books the right option into Jackrabbit, with staff approval before any booking goes through. The agent stops just capturing leads and starts placing kids into classes.

After-hours & weekend coverage

Extends beyond mornings once the core is proven.

Connected dashboards & data systems

Broader reporting across the gym’s systems, one place to see everything, once the underlying data flows are clean.

08What success means

The bar is the $600 break-even line from the calculator above. The pilot only needs to recover its own cost to be worth keeping, and as the calculator shows, that’s a low bar to clear. We set these tiers up front so the September call is straightforward:

SuccessfulAttributed revenue clears the $600 cost several times over, a clear positive ROI → expand
AverageRoughly breaks even, and visibly improves service & response speed → revise and continue
MediocreDoesn’t recover its cost and shows no service gain → stop

Exact revenue figures fill in once the pilot logs real data; the break-even bar itself ($600) is fixed and known today.
